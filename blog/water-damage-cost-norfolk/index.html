How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Norfolk?

Home / Blog / How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Norfolk?

The Quick Answer

In Norfolk, the usual water damage bill lands in a range of $1,500 to $10,000 or more. Small clean-water jobs can come in under about $1,500. Major flooding or a sewage backup that needs rebuilding can run well above $10,000.

Those numbers describe what is typical nationally; they are not a quote. Local labor rates and material prices in Norfolk, VA move the numbers up or down. The only way to get an exact figure is an on-site inspection with moisture readings.

Cost by Water Category

Restoration pricing usually starts with the type of water involved. The industry sorts water into three categories based on how contaminated it is. A job with dirty water calls for extra protective gear, more disinfecting and more material taken out.

Depending on which category applies, US cleanup costs are usually in the area of $3 to $7.50 a square foot. The table below shows common rates.

CategoryWhat it isTypical cost per sq ft
Category 1 (clean water)Water heater tanks, faucets and supply linesRoughly $3 to $4
Category 2, gray waterWashing machine or dishwasher discharge, sump pump failuresAbout $4 to $6.50
Category 3, black waterStorm surge, flooding rivers, sewageAbout $7 to $7.50

A loss does not always stay in one category. Leave clean water standing a couple of days and bacteria grow, so it gets handled as black or gray water. That is one reason waiting costs money.

What Drives the Price

The same kind of pipe failure may cost one homeowner much more than another. Here is what a contractor looks at when pricing a job.

  • Area affected. A larger wet area takes extra crew hours, additional machines and a longer stay.
  • Water category. Handling is least expensive when the water is clean. Sewage is the most expensive.
  • How long it sat. Given time, water works its way into wall cavities and subfloors. A leak that runs all weekend while you are gone costs far more than one caught in an hour.
  • Materials. Concrete and tile are easy to dry. Hardwood, carpet pad, drywall and insulation often need special drying or replacement.
  • Mold. Once mold is growing, the scope expands to include remediation, along with air filtration and containment.
  • Reconstruction. One part of the bill is drying. Replacing drywall, flooring, cabinets and paint afterward is a separate one, and on large jobs it is often the bigger of the two.

Most estimates are built around two phases: water extraction first, then structural drying. Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days.

A daily rate is common for dehumidifiers and air movers. A home that dries in three days costs less than one that takes six.

Typical Costs for Common Jobs

Below are the jobs our team handles most in Norfolk, with the national range that is typical for each. They are a place to begin, and an actual price will differ.

  • Burst pipe. Drying and cleanup frequently total $1,500 to $5,000, and the burst pipe repair is on top of that. Expect more if it ran for hours or soaked more than one floor.
  • Appliance leak. Found early, a leak from a dishwasher, water heater or washing machine is often $1,000 to $4,000. When a slow drip goes unnoticed below cabinets, concealed damage raises the price.
  • Basement flood. The usual range is $2,000 to $10,000 or more, varying with the water's source, its depth and any finished surfaces. Basement water removal in a finished basement sits at the high end.
  • Sewage backup. Many jobs cost $3,000 to $10,000, some more. Sewage cleanup is Category 3 work, so porous materials are removed and everything is disinfected.
  • Roof leak. Without the roof repair itself, drying and fixing walls, insulation and ceilings is often $1,000 to $5,000.

Does Insurance Cover Water Damage in Norfolk?

Two things decide it: the cause of the loss and your policy's wording. Standard homeowners policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental, such as a pipe that bursts or a supply line that fails without warning.

A leak that developed slowly is usually excluded. If a fitting dripped for months, an insurer may call it a maintenance issue. Flood water from outside, such as a rising river or storm surge, is usually excluded unless you carry separate flood insurance. Sewer backup often needs its own add-on.

Before assuming anything, read the policy or phone your agent. Remember to consider your deductible. If the work is minor, its cost may be below your deductible and there may be little point in a claim.

Keeping Your Costs Under Control

How much you pay for water damage is largely settled within a day or two. A few simple steps make a real difference.

  • Act fast. Water left in place goes on spreading. Every hour adds wet material.
  • Shut off the water. The moment you discover a leak, close the main shutoff, which means knowing in advance where to find it.
  • Document everything. Take photos and video before anything is moved or thrown out. Keep receipts.
  • Call a pro before mold starts. It can take as little as 24 to 48 hours for mold to start. After that point, expect added time and added cost.
  • Move what you can. Furniture, rugs and boxes should come up off wet flooring, provided that is safe.

Do not walk through standing water if the power is on, and stay out of anything that may contain sewage. No saving is worth a shock or an infection. If the water came from outside, leave flood cleanup to a crew with the right protective gear.

Getting a Real Number

A national range helps with budgeting, yet no house is the average house. To learn the true spread of the water, and the work required to dry and fix it, a technician has to inspect with a meter.
