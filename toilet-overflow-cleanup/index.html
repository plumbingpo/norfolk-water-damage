Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Norfolk: What to Know

An overflowing toilet seems like something towels can handle, except the water keeps moving past the tile. Some slips beneath the baseboard and into the wall, and some drops through the opening around the drain pipe. What matters most is what was in it. Clean water is what comes out of the supply line or the tank. Anything that rose out of the bowl is a different matter and must be treated as contaminated.

The first thing our Norfolk team does is identify the water category, since every later step depends on it. With clean water, the job is extraction followed by drying. Black or gray water calls for protective equipment, taking out porous materials that got soaked, carpet pad for example, and disinfecting everything it contacted. Finally we meter the subfloor, the bottoms of the walls and the space underneath, so no moisture gets left behind.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off the SupplyTurn the valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. If it will not turn, lift the tank lid and raise the float to stop the fill.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainHold off on flushing, and keep the washer, showers and sinks off too. If the drain line is blocked, any water you send down can come back up.
  3. Avoid Dirty WaterIf the water came from the bowl or contains waste, keep children and pets out, and wear gloves and waterproof shoes if you must walk through it.
  4. Check the Room BelowFor an upstairs toilet, look at the ceiling underneath. Should you see drips or a sag, cut the power to the room and get belongings out from under it.
  5. Block the SpreadLay down old towels so clean water does not travel into hallways or onto carpet. Contaminated water should be left to a crew that has the right disinfectants and equipment.
Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Clean water flooding the bathroom from a supply line that failed or a tank that cracked.
  • A clog in the trap or drain line that sends bowl water over the rim.
  • A child's toy, wipes or a wad of paper that gets flushed and sticks in the trap.
  • A sewer line narrowed by tree roots or a collapse, so every drain in the home runs slow.
  • Fill valves or flappers that stick, so the tank never stops running.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Subfloor Saturation

Water gets beneath tile and vinyl through grout lines and the toilet base. The plywood underneath swells, delaminates and can loosen the flooring bonded to it.

Bacteria in Building Materials

Bowl and drain water leaves bacteria in carpet, pad, baseboards and drywall. Because disinfecting porous material is not reliable, it usually has to come out.

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

Water from an upstairs bathroom runs down the drain pipe and sits on the drywall ceiling underneath. Stains and sagging follow, and now and then the ceiling comes down.

Odor That Returns

When contaminated water dries under flooring or in the base of a wall, the smell comes back in humid weather and stays until that material is removed.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

A sudden and accidental overflow, for example from an unexpected clog or a fill valve that failed, is covered by many homeowners policies. Unless a backup endorsement was added, policies often exclude water backing up out of a drain or the sewer, which may include an overflow that a blocked main line caused. Months of leaking around the base of a toilet usually falls outside coverage. Read your policy or call your agent to find out which situation applies to you.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • The type of bathroom flooring and whether it can be dried or must come up.
  • If fixtures such as the toilet or the vanity must come out to reach wet material below.
  • Whether the overflow stayed in the bathroom or reached carpet, closets or a hall outside it.
  • Whether the toilet is upstairs and the ceiling or walls below got wet.
