A dry house is not yet a finished house. Once the moisture readings are back to normal, there are still cut walls, missing flooring and bare subfloor to deal with. Water damage repair is the rebuild. Our team in Norfolk replaces what could not be saved and matches it to what was there before, so the room looks like a room again and not a job site.

The scope of repairs varies a lot. A minor loss can need just a couple of feet of wallboard plus baseboard and a coat of paint. On a large one it can mean insulation, full sheets of drywall, subfloor, hardwood or laminate, cabinet boxes, trim and doors. We replace materials like for like where possible, and we tell you plainly when an exact match is not available so you can choose.

Dry first, then cover: that order never changes. Before any new material goes on, we confirm with a moisture meter that framing and subfloor are at normal levels. Mold hidden in a newly painted wall usually traces back to someone covering damp lumber. You also receive a written scope and schedule ahead of the work, which spells out what gets rebuilt and when.

Warning Signs for Water Damage Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • You see brown stains, peeling or bubbles in paint along the path the water took.
  • Cabinet bases or side panels are swollen, soft or peeling at the bottom.
  • Baseboards and door casings are warped or no longer sit flat against the wall.
  • Grout is breaking up and some tiles have lifted where moisture reached the floor below.
  • Hardwood boards have cupped, crowned or lifted and did not flatten after drying.
  • Interior doors now stick or will not latch after swelling from moisture.
  • Following water from above, part of the ceiling droops, has cracks or crumbles when touched.

Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Repair

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • File the written scope and photos of the completed repair with your household papers.
  • Give fresh paint, caulk and flooring adhesive time to cure before wet mopping or heavy use.
  • Ask for final moisture readings before any contractor closes up a wall or floor.
  • Laundry rooms, bathrooms and kitchens should be rebuilt with moisture resistant wallboard and proper backer board.
  • Fresh caulk every year around showers, tubs and sinks stops water reaching the wall cavity.
  • Future repairs match better when you have held on to extra flooring, tile and the paint information.
